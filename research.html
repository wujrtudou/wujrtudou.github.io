Research

Statistical learning for data that are incomplete, heterogeneous and dynamic

Research pillar 01

Learning under complex data conditions

Modern data are often only partially labelled, selectively observed, contaminated or generated by multiple latent populations.

Core questions

  • How does informative missingness change what can be learned from labelled and unlabelled data?
  • When can partially observed data improve inference rather than weaken it?
  • How can robust procedures remain reliable under outliers, heavy tails and distributional heterogeneity?

Methods

  • Semi-supervised learning and finite mixture models
  • Informative and favourable missingness mechanisms
  • Robust estimation, kernel methods and regularisation

Research pillar 02

Computational statistics and optimisation

Methodological advances only become useful when they can be fitted reliably and efficiently.

Core questions

  • How can difficult high-dimensional and non-convex objectives be solved more efficiently?
  • How should hyperparameters be selected without excessive tuning or unstable validation?
  • How can iterative and stochastic algorithms balance speed, accuracy and robustness?

Methods

  • Iterative and stochastic optimisation
  • Evolutionary and swarm-based algorithms
  • Automatic hyperparameter selection and scalable computation

Research pillar 03

Forecasting, uncertainty and causal dynamics

Forecasts should communicate uncertainty and remain useful when systems are disrupted, attacked or structurally changing.

Core questions

  • How can deterministic and probabilistic forecasts remain calibrated under changing regimes?
  • How can physical knowledge be combined with statistical and machine-learning models?
  • How can interventions and causal mechanisms be studied in dynamic systems?

Applications

  • Power systems, electricity demand and integrated energy systems
  • Marine, hydrological, groundwater and air-quality forecasting
  • Environmental, geoscience and socio-economic systems

Current directions

Research priorities

Current work extends these themes into trustworthy AI, physics-informed learning and uncertainty-aware prediction.

A

Informative missingness in semi-supervised inference

Understanding when the mechanism that determines what is observed carries information that should be modelled directly.

B

Physics-informed learning with imperfect constraints

Combining governing structure with noisy, incomplete or large-scale observations in environmental and engineering systems.

C

Probabilistic forecasting for interconnected systems

Developing models that quantify uncertainty across energy, groundwater, climate and environmental applications.

D

Trustworthy and responsible scientific AI

Examining robustness, transparency and responsible deployment of AI in geoscience and other scientific domains.

Research collaboration

Methodological depth, interdisciplinary reach

I am particularly interested in collaborations where statistical structure, computational difficulty and domain knowledge must be addressed together.
